🚨 1. Part XVIII: Emergency Provisions & 3 Types of Emergency

Emergency provisions are contained in Part XVIII (Articles 352 to 360) of the Constitution. Suspension of Fundamental Rights during emergency is borrowed from the Weimar Constitution of Germany.

1. National Emergency (Art 352)

Proclaimed due to War, External Aggression, or Armed Rebellion. Approved by Parliament within 1 month (Special Majority).

2. President's Rule (Art 356)

State / Constitutional Emergency on failure of constitutional machinery. Approved within 2 months (Simple Majority).

3. Financial Emergency (Art 360)

Threat to financial stability or credit of India. Approved within 2 months (Simple Majority). Never imposed till date.

⚡ 2. Article 352: National Emergency & 44th Amendment Act Safeguards

Proclamation & Grounds
  • Proclaimed by President ONLY on written advice of the Union Cabinet (44th CA 1978).
  • ⭐ The word "Cabinet" is mentioned ONLY in Article 352(3) in the entire Constitution.
  • 3 Grounds:
    1. War (युद्ध)
    2. External Aggression (बाह्य आक्रमण)
    3. Armed Rebellion (सशस्त्र विद्रोह — Replaced "Internal Disturbance" via 44th CA 1978).
  • Minerva Mills Case (1980): Proclamation is subject to Judicial Review.
Approval, Duration & Revocation
  • Must be approved within 1 Month by both Houses with Special Majority (Majority of total membership + 2/3rd present & voting).
  • Valid for 6 Months; can be extended indefinitely with 6-monthly Parliamentary approval.
  • Revocation: By President anytime, OR if Lok Sabha passes a disapproval resolution by Simple Majority (1/10th MPs can demand special sitting within 14 days).

🛡️ 3. Effects on Centre-State Relations, FRs (Arts 358–359) & 3 Historical Wars

1. Centre-State Relations

Federal structure becomes Unitary. Centre can give directions on ANY matter. Parliament can make laws on State List (valid up to 6 months after emergency ends).

2. Life of Lok Sabha

Tenure of Lok Sabha and State Assemblies can be extended by Parliament for 1 year at a time (max 6 months after emergency ceases).

3. Articles 358 vs 359

• Art 358: Auto suspension of Art 19 (External emergency only).
• Art 359: President suspends right to move court.
⭐ Arts 20 & 21 CAN NEVER BE SUSPENDED!

National Emergencies Declared in India Till Date (3 Times):

1. 1962–1968: Indo-China War

Declared in Oct 1962 during Chinese aggression in NEFA (Arunachal Pradesh). PM: Jawaharlal Nehru.

2. 1971–1977: Indo-Pak War

Declared in Dec 1971 during war leading to liberation of Bangladesh. PM: Indira Gandhi.

3. 1975–1977: Internal Disturbance

Declared on 25th June 1975 (*"Samvidhan Hatya Diwas"*). PM: Indira Gandhi, President: Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed.

🏛️ 4. Articles 355 & 356: President's Rule & S.R. Bommai Case (1994)

Article 355 & 356 Grounds & Approval
  • Art 355: Duty of Union to protect States against external aggression & internal disturbance.
  • Art 356: Proclaimed on breakdown of constitutional machinery in State (also under Art 365 if State defies Centre's directions).
  • Approval: Within 2 Months by both Houses by Simple Majority.
  • Duration: 6 Months at a time; Maximum Period = 3 Years.
  • No effect on Fundamental Rights during President's Rule.
Historical Facts & S.R. Bommai Case
  • 1st Imposed: In Punjab (1951).
  • Max Times: Manipur (11 times), Uttar Pradesh (10 times).
  • ⭐ S.R. Bommai Case (1994): Held that Art 356 is subject to Judicial Review; Assembly cannot be dissolved until Parliament approves.
  • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar: Wished Art 356 to remain a "Dead Letter" (used only as last resort).

💰 5. Article 360: Financial Emergency, Inter-State Council & Zonal Councils

Article 360 : Financial Emergency
  • Proclaimed when financial stability or credit of India is threatened.
  • Approved within 2 Months by Parliament by Simple Majority.
  • Salaries of government servants and SC/HC Judges can be reduced.
  • ⭐ Never imposed in India till date.
Inter-State Council [Article 263]
  • Established in 1990 on Sarkaria Commission recommendation.
  • Chairman: Prime Minister.
  • Members: CMs of all States & UTs + Administrators + 6 Union Cabinet Ministers.
Zonal Councils (States Reorganisation Act 1956):

Statutory Bodies (5 Zones: Northern, Central, Eastern, Western, Southern). North Eastern Council created in 1971.
• Chairman: Union Home Minister.
• Vice-Chairman: CMs of member states by rotation for 1 year.

Centre-State Relations Commissions:
  • • Rajamannar Commission (1969): By Tamil Nadu Govt.
  • • Sarkaria Commission (1983): Recommended Inter-State Council.
  • • Punchhi Commission (2007): Headed by Justice M.M. Punchhi.

📜 6. Part XX: Constitutional Amendment (Article 368) & Basic Structure

Basic Pointers & Procedure
  • Borrowed from Constitution of South Africa.
  • Introduced in either House of Parliament (LS or RS); CANNOT be introduced in State Legislatures.
  • Can be introduced by a Minister or Private Member.
  • No prior recommendation of President required.
  • 🚫 NO provision for Joint Sitting for Amendment Bills.
  • ⭐ President MUST give assent (24th CA 1971 removed veto power over amendment bills).
Basic Structure Doctrine (मूल ढांचा सिद्धांत)

Born in landmark Kesavananda Bharati Case (24 April 1973). Parliament cannot alter or destroy the Basic Structure of the Constitution.
Includes: Supremacy of Constitution, Judicial Review, Secularism, Federalism, Rule of Law, Separation of Powers, Free & Fair Elections.

📊 7. 3 Types of Majorities & Recent Landmark Amendments (100th to 106th)

1. Simple Majority (Outside 368)

Admission/formation of new states (Arts 2, 3), Legislative Councils (Art 169), 2nd/5th/6th Schedules, Quorum, MP salaries, Official language.

2. Special Majority (Art 368)

Majority of total membership + 2/3rd present & voting. Covers: Fundamental Rights (Part III) & DPSP (Part IV).

3. Special + 1/2 States Ratification

Federal provisions: Election of President, Supreme Court/High Courts, 7th Schedule lists, Representation in Parliament, Art 368 itself.

Recent Constitutional Amendments (100th to 106th CA):

100th CA (2015): India-Bangladesh Land Boundary Agreement (LBA)
101st CA (2016): Goods and Services Tax (GST) & Article 279A
102nd CA (2018): Constitutional Status to NCBC (Article 338B)
103rd CA (2019): 10% EWS Reservation (Articles 15(6) & 16(6))
104th CA (2020): Abolished Anglo-Indian nomination; SC/ST extension
105th CA (2021): Restored State power to identify SEBCs
⭐ 106th CA (2023 - Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam): 1/3rd (33%) seats reserved for Women in Lok Sabha & State Assemblies (Arts 330A, 332A)

🤝 8. 3 Dimensions of Centre-State Relations (Parts XI & XII)

1. Legislative Relations

Part XI (Articles 245 to 255): Territorial extent of laws & 7th Schedule (Union, State & Concurrent Lists; Residuary powers to Centre).

2. Administrative Relations

Part XI (Articles 256 to 263): Executive directions by Centre to States, construction of communications, Inter-State Council (Art 263).

3. Financial Relations

Part XII (Articles 268 to 293): Distribution of tax revenues, Grants-in-Aid (Art 275), Finance Commission (Art 280), GST sharing.

🗣️ 9. Part XVII: Official Languages of the Union & States (Articles 343 to 351)

Union & Parliamentary Languages
  • Art 343: Official Language of Union is Hindi in Devanagari script with international form of Indian numerals.
  • Art 344: Official Language Commission (B.G. Kher in 1955) & Committee (30 MPs: 20 LS + 10 RS).
  • Art 345: Official language(s) of a State.
  • Art 348: Language of Supreme Court, High Courts & authoritative texts of Acts shall be in English.
Special Directives & Linguistic Safeguards
  • Art 350: Representation for redress of grievances in any language.
  • Art 350A: Facilities for instruction in Mother Tongue at Primary Stage for linguistic minorities.
  • Art 350B: Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities appointed by President (7th CA 1956).
  • Art 351: Directive for the development and spread of Hindi Language.
