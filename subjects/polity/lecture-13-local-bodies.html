🌱 1. Evolution of Panchayati Raj System & Landmark Committees

1. Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957)

Recommended 3-tier Panchayati Raj System:
1. Gram Panchayat (Village Level)
2. Panchayat Samiti (Block Level)
3. Zila Parishad (District Level)
⭐ 1st State: Rajasthan (Nagaur district, 2 Oct 1959) by Nehru, followed by Andhra Pradesh (11 Oct 1959).

2. Ashok Mehta Committee (1977)

Proposed 2-tier Panchayati Raj System:
1. Zila Parishad (Main executive unit at district level)
2. Mandal Panchayat (Cluster of villages with 15k–20k population).

3. G.V.K. Rao Committee (1985)

Reviewed rural development administration. Termed PRIs as "Grass without roots". Proposed Zila Parishad as nodal agency.

4. L. M. Singhvi Committee (1986)

⭐ 1st to recommend CONSTITUTIONAL STATUS for Panchayati Raj Institutions. Highlighted Gram Sabha as embodiment of Direct Democracy and recommended Nyaya Panchayats.

5. Thungon Committee (1988) & Gadgil Committee (1988):

Recommended constitutional status, 5-year fixed tenure, direct elections at all 3 levels, and reservation for SC, ST & Women.

📜 2. 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 (Part IX)

Key Constitutional Facts
  • Inserted Part IX (Articles 243 to 243-O).
  • Added 11th Schedule with 29 Functional Items.
  • Enforced on: 24th April 1993 (Celebrated as National Panchayati Raj Day).
  • Prime Minister during enactment: P. V. Narasimha Rao.
Democratic Decentralisation Pillars
  • Fulfilled Mahatma Gandhi's vision in DPSP Article 40 (Organization of Village Panchayats).
  • Created 3-tier structure: Village, Block & District.
  • Mandatory regular elections every 5 years by State Election Commission.

🏛️ 3. Articles 243 to 243E: Structure, Reservation & Duration

Article 243A : Gram Sabha

Consists of all registered voters in the village. Village-level parliament exercising direct democracy.

Article 243B : 3-Tier System

Mandatory 3 tiers. Exemption: States with population ≤ 20 Lakhs may not have intermediate (block) tier.

Article 243C : Composition

All seats filled by Direct Election. Block & District Chairpersons elected indirectly.

Article 243D : Reservation of Seats

  • • SC / ST Reservation: In proportion to their population in the area.
  • • Women Reservation: Mandatory minimum 1/3rd (33%) of total seats and chairpersons.

Article 243E : Duration (5 Years & Remainder Rule)

  • • Fixed term of 5 years from date of first meeting.
  • • If dissolved early, election within 6 months.
  • • Reconstituted Panchayat serves ONLY for the remainder of the 5-year term.

💰 4. Articles 243F to 243M: 21 Yrs Age, Finance Commission & Exempted Areas

Article 243F : Min Age 21 Yrs

Minimum age to contest Panchayat election is 21 Years (Cannot be disqualified if 21+ years of age).

Article 243-I : State Finance Comm.

Constituted by Governor every 5 years to review financial position & recommend tax sharing.

Article 243K : State Election Comm.

SEC appointed by Governor; removed in the same manner as a High Court Judge.

Article 243M: Areas Exempted from Part IX (पंचायती राज लागू नहीं):
  • 1. States of Nagaland, Meghalaya, and Mizoram (Traditional tribal councils exist).
  • 2. Hill areas of Manipur (District Councils exist).
  • 3. Delhi (UT): Delhi is the ONLY Union Territory where Panchayats do NOT exist.

💡 5. The PESA Act, 1996 & Super Mnemonic Trick for Articles 243 to 243-O

The PESA Act, 1996 (Panchayats Extension to Scheduled Areas Act):

Enacted on recommendations of Bhuria Committee (1995) to extend Part IX provisions to Fifth Schedule Areas (10 States), empowering Gram Sabhas with self-rule and ownership over minor forest produce & water bodies.

🏙️ 6. 74th Amendment Act, 1992 & Lord Ripon's Magna Carta

74th CAA, 1992 (Part IX-A)
  • Inserted Part IX-A (Articles 243P to 243ZG).
  • Added 12th Schedule with 18 Functional Items.
  • Enforced on: 1st June 1993.
Historical Evolution of Urban Bodies
  • 1st Municipal Corp (1687–88): Madras.
  • 1726: Bombay and Calcutta Corporations.
  • 1870: Lord Mayo's Financial Decentralisation.
  • 1882: Lord Ripon's Resolution — "Magna Carta of Local Self-Gov" (Lord Ripon = Father of Local Self-Gov).

🏢 7. 8 Types of Urban Local Bodies & 3 Types of Municipalities

1. Nagar Panchayat

For a Transitional Area (rural to urban transition, e.g. Kalyanpur).

2. Municipal Council

For a Smaller Urban Area (Nagar Palika Parishad, e.g. Chhindwara).

3. Municipal Corporation

For a Larger Urban Area (Nagar Nigam, e.g. Indore, Delhi, Mumbai).

8 Types of Urban Local Government in India:

1. Municipal Corporation
2. Municipality
3. Notified Area Comm.
4. Cantonment Board (Defence)
5. Township
6. Town Area Committee
7. Port Trust
8. Special Purpose Agency
Article 243ZD : District Planning Committee (DPC)

Consolidates plans prepared by Panchayats and Municipalities in the district.

Article 243ZE : Metropolitan Planning Committee (MPC)

Prepares draft development plan for metropolitan area (population ≥ 10 Lakhs).

🤝 8. Part IX-B: The Co-operative Societies (97th CA 2011)

97th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011 (Articles 243ZH to 243ZT)

  • • Inserted Part IX-B (Articles 243ZH–243ZT) giving constitutional status to Co-operatives.
  • • Made right to form co-operative societies a Fundamental Right [Article 19(1)(c)].
  • • Added Article 43B in DPSP (Promotion of Co-operative Societies).
  • • Board of Directors: Max 21 directors; 1 SC/ST + 2 Women seats reserved; 5-year term.
