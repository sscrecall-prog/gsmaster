📜 1. Article 107: Introduction, Types of Bills & Lapsing on Dissolution

4 Types of Bills in Parliament
  • 1. Ordinary Bill (साधारण विधेयक): Originates in either House; simple majority.
  • 2. Money Bill (धन विधेयक) [Art 110]: Originates ONLY in Lok Sabha with President's recommendation.
  • 3. Financial Bill (वित्तीय विधेयक) [Art 117]: Categories (I) and (III).
  • 4. Constitutional Amendment Bill [Art 368]: Special majority in both Houses.
• Public Bill: Introduced by a Minister (7 days notice).
• Private Member's Bill: Introduced by non-Minister MP (1 month notice).
Article 107(4) : Lapsing of Bills on LS Dissolution
  • ❌ Bill pending in Lok Sabha ➔ LAPSES.
  • ❌ Bill passed by LS but pending in RS ➔ LAPSES.
  • ✅ Bill pending in RS but NOT passed by LS ➔ DOES NOT LAPSE.
  • ✅ Bill passed by both Houses pending President's assent ➔ DOES NOT LAPSE.
  • ✅ Joint Sitting summoned by President before dissolution ➔ DOES NOT LAPSE.

🏛️ 2. Article 108: Joint Sitting of Both Houses

Deadlock Conditions (3 Cases):

When an Ordinary Bill passed by one House is: (1) Rejected by the other House, (2) Houses disagree on amendments, or (3) More than 6 months elapse without the bill being passed.

Procedure & Hierarchy
  • Summoned by the President; provision borrowed from Australia.
  • Presiding Officer Hierarchy:
    1. Speaker of Lok Sabha
    2. Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha
    3. Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha
    (RS Chairman / VP CANNOT preside as he is not a member of Parliament).
  • Passed by a Simple Majority of total members present and voting.
  • Follows the Rules of Procedure of Lok Sabha.
Historic Joint Sittings (3 Times) & Exceptions

Joint Sittings held in India:

  • 1. Dowry Prohibition Bill, 1960 (May 1961)
  • 2. Banking Services Commission (Repeal) Bill, 1977 (May 1978)
  • 3. Prevention of Terrorism Bill (POTA), 2002 (March 2002)
🚫 Exceptions (NO Joint Sitting allowed):
• Money Bills [Art 110]
• Constitutional Amendment Bills [Art 368]

💰 3. Articles 109 & 110: Money Bills & Special Procedure

Article 109 : Special Procedure for Money Bills
  • Introduced ONLY in Lok Sabha on the prior recommendation of the President.
  • Rajya Sabha has restricted powers: cannot reject or amend; can only make recommendations within 14 days.
  • If not returned within 14 days, it is deemed passed by both Houses in original Lok Sabha form.
Article 110 : Definition of Money Bill
  • • Imposition, abolition, remission, alteration of any tax.
  • • Borrowing of money / financial obligations by Union.
  • • Custody of Consolidated Fund or Contingency Fund.
  • • Appropriation of money from Consolidated Fund.
  • • Declaring any expenditure as charged on Consolidated Fund.
📌 Speaker's Certification & Judicial Review:

The Speaker of Lok Sabha certifies whether a Bill is a Money Bill or not, and his decision is final. A bill imposing fines/penalties or local taxes by municipal bodies is NOT a Money Bill.

🛡️ 4. Article 111: Assent to Bills & 3 Presidential Veto Powers

1. Suspensive Veto (निलंबनकारी)

President returns bill for reconsideration. If passed again by Parliament (simple majority), President MUST give assent. *(Not available for Money Bills).*

2. Pocket Veto (जेबी वीटो)

President keeps bill pending indefinitely. Indian President's pocket is deeper than US President's (no constitution time limit). *(Used by Giani Zail Singh in 1986 on Post Office Bill).*

3. Absolute Veto (आत्यंतिक)

President withholds assent completely (Bill ends). Used for private members' bills or when cabinet resigns before assent.

⭐ 24th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1971:

Made Presidential assent mandatory for Constitutional Amendment Bills under Article 368 (President has NO veto power over Constitution amendments).

📊 5. Articles 112–114: Annual Financial Statement (Budget) & Cut Motions

Article 112 : Annual Financial Statement (वार्षिक वित्तीय विवरण)

Estimated receipts and expenditures laid before Parliament. Note: The word 'Budget' is nowhere mentioned in the Constitution.

Article 113: Estimates & 3 Cut Motions (Moved in Lok Sabha on Demands for Grants):

1. Policy Cut (नीतिगत)

"Amount be reduced to ₹1" — Disapproves the underlying government policy.

2. Economy Cut (मितव्ययिता)

"Amount be reduced by a specified sum" — Suggests savings in proposed expenditure.

3. Token Cut (सांकेतिक)

"Amount be reduced by ₹100" — Expresses a specific grievance within GoI's sphere.

🏦 6. Articles 115–117 & The Three Central Funds of India

1. Consolidated Fund of India [Art 266(1)]

All revenue receipts, loans, and loan recoveries. Money withdrawn ONLY through Parliamentary Appropriation Act.

2. Public Account of India [Art 266(2)]

Provident funds, judicial deposits, savings bank remittances. Operated by Executive action (no vote needed).

3. Contingency Fund of India [Art 267]

Disaster/unforeseen expenses. Held by President, operated by Finance Secretary (₹30,000 Cr corpus).

Supplementary Grant (115): Insufficient budget allocation.
Vote on Account (116): 2 months advance to Executive pending budget.
Vote of Credit (116): "Blank Cheque" for unexpected urgent demands.

⚡ 7. Article 123: Power of President to Promulgate Ordinances

Conditions & Nature of Ordinance
  • Issued when both Houses or either House is not in session.
  • Has the same force and effect as an Act of Parliament.
  • Cannot violate Fundamental Rights or amend Constitution.
  • Can be withdrawn anytime by the President.
Maximum Life of an Ordinance
  • Must be approved within 6 weeks (42 days) from reassembly of Parliament.
  • Maximum Life: 6 Months + 6 Weeks (Since max gap between two sessions is 6 months).
  • Art 123 for President (Centre) vs Art 213 for Governor (State).

🔴 8. Special Exclusive Powers of Rajya Sabha (Arts 249 & 312)

Article 249 : Legislation on State List

Rajya Sabha can authorize Parliament to make laws on a subject in the State List in national interest by passing a resolution supported by 2/3rd majority of members present and voting (valid for 1 year).

Article 312 : Creation of All India Services

Rajya Sabha can authorize Parliament to create one or more new All India Services (AIS) common to Union and States by passing a resolution supported by 2/3rd majority present and voting.

👥 9. Top 3 Financial Standing Committees of Parliament

1. Public Accounts Committee (PAC)

Est: 1921 (GoI Act 1919).
Members: 22 (15 Lok Sabha + 7 Rajya Sabha).
Chairman: From Opposition (since 1967).
Function: Audits CAG reports. CAG is "Guide, Friend & Philosopher" of PAC.

2. Estimates Committee

Est: 1950 (John Mathai Committee).
Members: 30 (ALL 30 from Lok Sabha).
Largest Parliamentary Committee.
Function: Suggests economies in budget (Continuous Economy Committee).

3. Committee on Public Undertakings

Est: 1964 (Krishna Menon Committee).
Members: 22 (15 Lok Sabha + 7 Rajya Sabha).
Chairman: From Lok Sabha.
Function: Examines accounts and CAG reports of PSUs.

Common Rules of Financial Committees:

Elected by Proportional Representation with STV • Term: 1 Year • NO Minister can be a member of any Financial Committee.

📋 10. Departmental Standing Committees & Speaker's Chairmanships

Departmental Standing Committees (DRSCs)
  • Total: 24 Committees (16 under Lok Sabha + 8 under Rajya Sabha).
  • Strength: 31 Members each (21 from Lok Sabha + 10 from Rajya Sabha).
  • Examine Demands for Grants and Bills related to concerned ministries.
Speaker & Deputy Speaker Special Rules
  • Speaker is Ex-officio Chairman of:
    • Business Advisory Committee (BAC)
    • Rules Committee
    • General Purposes Committee
  • Deputy Speaker: If nominated to any committee, he automatically becomes its Chairman.
