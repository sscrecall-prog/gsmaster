Indian Courts & Judiciary

🏛️ 1. Basic Pointers & Single Integrated Judicial System

Structure & Historical Origin
  • Judiciary in India is Integrated (borrowed from GoI Act, 1935) and Independent (borrowed from USA).
  • Federal Court established in 1937 under GoI Act 1935.
  • Supreme Court replaced Federal Court & Privy Council (London).
  • 1st Sitting of Supreme Court: 28 January 1950.
  • Supreme Court is 'The Guardian of the Constitution'.
Pyramid Structure of Courts
🏛️ Supreme Court of India (Apex Court - Top)
🏢 25 High Courts (State Level)
👥 Subordinate Courts (District & Sessions Courts)

⚖️ 2. Article 124: Establishment, Composition & Qualifications of SC Judges

Strength & Appointment
  • Originally (1950): 1 CJI + 7 Judges = 8 Judges.
  • Present Strength: 1 CJI + 33 Judges = 34 Judges (Max Sanctioned).
  • Parliament increases strength by law.
  • Appointed by the President in consultation with CJI and Collegium.
  • Tenure: Serves till the age of 65 years.
  • Resignation addressed to the President.
Qualifications for SC Judge [Art 124(3)]
  • Must be a Citizen of India; AND
  • (a) At least 5 years as High Court Judge; OR
  • (b) At least 10 years as High Court Advocate; OR
  • (c) Distinguished Jurist in opinion of President.
• Restriction [124(7)]: Retired SC judge cannot practice in any court in India.

📜 3. Removal Procedure (Judges Inquiry Act 1968) & 4 Judges Cases

Removal of Judges [Art 124(4)]
  • Grounds: 1. Proved Misbehaviour or 2. Incapacity.
  • Passed by Special Majority in both Houses of Parliament.
  • Judges Inquiry Act, 1968: Motion signed by 100 members in Lok Sabha OR 50 members in Rajya Sabha.
  • 3-member judicial committee investigates.
  • ⭐ No Supreme Court Judge has been removed till date.
Historic CJI & Women Judges Facts
  • 1st Chief Justice of India: Sir Harilal Jekisundas Kania (H.J. Kania).
  • 1st Female Judge of SC: Justice M. Fathima Beevi (1989).
  • No female Chief Justice of India till date.
  • 52nd CJI: Justice B. R. Gavai | 53rd CJI: Justice Surya Kant.

Evolution of Collegium System (4 Landmark Judges Cases):

1st Case (1982)

S.P. Gupta Case: Consultation does NOT mean concurrence; Executive primacy.

2nd Case (1993)

Consultation = Concurrence. Collegium born: CJI + 2 senior judges (Binding).

3rd Case (1998)

Collegium expanded to 5 members: CJI + 4 senior-most judges.

4th Case (2015)

Struck down 99th CA & NJAC Act as unconstitutional; restored Collegium.

📌 4. Articles 125 to 132: Appointments, Court of Record & Original Jurisdiction

Article 126 : Acting CJI

President appoints Acting Chief Justice when CJI office is vacant or CJI is unable to perform duties.

Article 127 : Ad Hoc Judges

CJI with President's consent requests a qualified High Court Judge to sit as temporary Ad Hoc judge for quorum.

Article 128 : Retired Judges

Retired SC/HC judges can be invited by CJI with President's consent to sit and act as SC judge.

Article 129 : Court of Record (अभिलेख न्यायालय)

Judgments preserved as legal precedents. Has power to punish for Contempt of Court (Civil & Criminal contempt).

Article 131 : Exclusive Original Jurisdiction

• Centre vs States | Centre + States vs Other States | Inter-State Disputes.
🚫 Exceptions: Pre-const treaties, Inter-state water disputes (Art 262), Finance Commission.

⚡ 5. Articles 133 to 145: SLP (136), Complete Justice (142) & Advisory (143)

Article 136 : Special Leave (SLP)

Discretionary power to grant leave to appeal from any court/tribunal in India. (Military Courts/Tribunals excluded).

Article 137 : Review Power

Supreme Court can review its own judgments/orders. Judicial review borrowed from USA.

Article 141 : Law Binding

Law declared by the Supreme Court is binding on all courts within the territory of India.

Article 142 : Complete Justice (पूर्ण न्याय)

Supreme Court can pass any decree or order necessary for doing complete justice in any cause or matter pending before it.

Article 143 : Advisory Jurisdiction

President refers matters to SC: (1) Public importance (SC may/may not advise), (2) Pre-constitutional treaties (SC MUST advise). Advice is NOT binding on President.

💡 6. Super Memory Trick for Supreme Court Articles (124 to 131)

🏢 7. High Courts in States (Part VI: Articles 214–222 & NHRC Trick)

Article 217: HC Judges Appointment & Tenure
  • Appointed by President (Consultation: CJI + Governor + HC Chief Justice).
  • Tenure: Serves till the age of 62 years.
  • Resigns to the President; removed like SC judge.
  • Qualifications: Citizen + 10 yrs advocate in HC / 10 yrs judicial office.
  • ❌ No provision for "Distinguished Jurist" in High Court.
Important Facts & Salary Difference
  • 1st HC (1862): Calcutta High Court.
  • 1st Female HC Judge: Justice Anna Chandy (Kerala).
  • 1st Female Chief Justice of HC: Justice Leila Seth (HP).
  • Total High Courts in India: 25 High Courts.
  • 💰 Salaries: From Consolidated Fund of State.
    👵 Pensions: From Consolidated Fund of India.
💡 "NHRC AAO ReST karo" (Articles 214 to 222)

🛡️ 8. Article 226 (Writ Jurisdiction) & Common High Courts (7th CA 1956)

Article 226 : High Court Writ Jurisdiction (Wider than SC)

  • • HC can issue writs for Fundamental Rights + Legal Rights.
  • • SC (Art 32) issues writs ONLY for Fundamental Rights.
  • • L. Chandra Kumar Case (1997): Judicial review under Arts 226 & 32 is part of the Basic Structure.

Article 231 : Common High Courts

  • • Guwahati HC: Assam, Nagaland, Mizoram, Arunachal (4 States).
  • • Bombay HC: Maharashtra, Goa, DNHDD.
  • • Calcutta HC: WB + Andaman & Nicobar.
  • • Kerala HC: Kerala + Lakshadweep.

👥 9. Subordinate Courts (Articles 233–237) & District Judges

Article 233 : Appointment of District Judges
  • Appointed by the Governor of State in consultation with High Court.
  • Direct recruitment qualification: Minimum 7 years as an advocate + recommended by High Court.
Articles 234 & 235 : Control & Other Judges
  • Art 234: Recruitment of other judicial officers by Governor with SPSC & HC.
  • Art 235: Control (posting, promotion, leave) over Subordinate Courts vests in High Court.

🤝 10. Lok Adalats & National Legal Services Authority (NALSA)

Status & NALSA Hierarchy
  • Alternative Dispute Redressal (ADR) mechanism for amicable settlement.
  • Statutory Status: Under Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987.
  • NALSA Patron-in-Chief: Chief Justice of India (CJI).
  • NALSA Executive Chairman: Senior-most Judge of Supreme Court.
Key Rules: No Fees & Binding Award
  • 💰 Fees: NO court fee payable (existing court fee refunded).
  • ⚖️ Award: Deemed as decree of Civil Court; Final & Binding.
  • 🚫 Appeal: NO appeal lies against the award before any court.
  • 🚫 Excluded Cases: Non-compoundable criminal offences CANNOT be dealt with.
