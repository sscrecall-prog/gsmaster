📜 1. Prime Minister & Council of Ministers (Articles 74–78)

Constitutional Position:
  • The Prime Minister is the Head of the Union Government and leader of the Council of Ministers.
  • The PM is the Real / De facto Head of the Government (President is the De jure / Nominal Head).
संवैधानिक स्थिति:
संसदीय शासन प्रणाली में राष्ट्रपति राज्य/देश का संवैधानिक (नाममात्र का) प्रमुख होता है, जबकि प्रधानमंत्री सरकार का वास्तविक (De facto) प्रमुख होता है।

Key Bodies Chaired by the Prime Minister of India:

1. NITI Aayog

Ex-officio Chairman of National Institution for Transforming India (Replaced Planning Commission in 2015).

2. Inter-State Council

Ex-officio Chairman of Inter-State Council under Article 263 to coordinate Centre-State relations.

3. NDMA

Chairman of National Disaster Management Authority for apex disaster policy & response.

4. National Integration Council

Heads National Integration Council to address communalism, regionalism and casteism.

5. National Water Resources

Chairman of National Water Resources Council for integrated national water policy.

6. Appointments Committee

Chairman of Appointments Committee of Cabinet (ACC) for senior civil & military postings.

🏛️ 2. Article 74: Council of Ministers to Aid & Advise President

Article 74(1) : Aid and Advice

There shall be a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head to aid and advise the President who shall act in accordance with such advice.

• 42nd CA (1976): Made ministerial advice binding on the President.
• 44th CA (1978): President may return advice once for reconsideration; but after reconsideration, the President must accept the advice.
Article 74(2) : Bar on Court Inquiry

The question whether any, and if so what, advice was tendered by Ministers to the President shall NOT be inquired into by any court (Confidentiality of advice).

📌 Origin of the Term "Cabinet":
The word "Cabinet" was not in original Constitution. 44th Amendment Act, 1978 introduced it formally into Article 352(3) (National Emergency on written recommendation of the Cabinet).

⚖️ 3. Article 75: Appointment, Collective Responsibility & 91st CA

Article 75(1) : Appointment

Prime Minister is appointed by the President, and other Ministers are appointed by President on the advice of PM.

Article 75(2) : Individual Responsibility

Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President (PM can ask any minister to resign or recommend dismissal).

Article 75(3) : Collective Responsibility ⭐

The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the House of the People (Lok Sabha). If a No-Confidence Motion is passed in Lok Sabha, all ministers must resign (They sink or swim together).

Article 75(4) : Oath of Office & Secrecy

Administered by the President in the forms set out in the Third Schedule.

Article 75(5) : Six Months Rule

A Minister who is not a member of either House for 6 consecutive months ceases to be a Minister at the end of that period.

Article 75(6) : Salaries & Allowances

Determined by Parliament by law; until then, specified in the Second Schedule.

⭐ 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003:
  • Article 75(1A): The total number of Ministers, including the Prime Minister, in the Council of Ministers shall NOT exceed 15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha.
  • Article 75(1B): A member disqualified on grounds of defection (10th Schedule) shall also be disqualified from being appointed as a Minister.

👥 4. Three Categories of Ministers

1. Cabinet Ministers

Hold crucial portfolios like Defence, Home, Finance, External Affairs, Education.

Attend and participate in all Cabinet meetings; determine core national policy.
2. Ministers of State (MoS)

Can be given Independent Charge of smaller ministries or assist Cabinet Ministers.

Do not participate in Cabinet meetings unless specially invited.
3. Deputy Ministers

Attached to Cabinet Ministers or Ministers of State to assist in administrative duties.

No independent charge; lowest tier in the ministerial hierarchy.

🛡️ 5. Article 76: Attorney General of India & Advocate General (Art 165)

Article 76 : Attorney General of India (महान्यायवादी)
  • Highest Law Officer of the Country (देश का सर्वोच्च विधिक अधिकारी).
  • Appointed by the President; must be qualified to be a Judge of the Supreme Court.
  • Holds office during the pleasure of the President.
  • Article 76(3): Has the Right of Audience in all courts in India.
  • Article 88: Right to speak and participate in Parliament proceedings, WITHOUT the right to vote.
  • Not a government servant; not debarred from private practice.
Article 165 : Advocate General of State (महाधिवक्ता)
  • Highest Law Officer of the State (राज्य का सर्वोच्च कानून अधिकारी).
  • Appointed by the Governor; must be qualified to be a Judge of a High Court.
  • Holds office during the pleasure of the Governor.
  • Article 177: Right to speak in State Legislature without right to vote.
1st Attorney General:
M. C. Setalvad (Longest serving, 1950–1963)
Current Attorney General:
R. Venkataramani (16th AG)
Solicitor General of India:
Tushar Mehta (Statutory post, assists AG)

⚡ 6. Dissolution of CoM & Rajya Sabha Members who became PM

Dissolution Rules of Council of Ministers:
  • If an individual Minister resigns or dies ➔ only a vacancy is created.
  • If the Prime Minister dies or resigns ➔ the entire Council of Ministers is automatically dissolved.
  • First PM to resign: Morarji Desai (1979).
Rajya Sabha Members who Became PM:

1. Indira Gandhi (1966)

2. H. D. Deve Gowda (1996)

3. I. K. Gujral (1997)

4. Dr. Manmohan Singh (2004, 2009)

📋 7. Articles 77 & 78: Conduct of Business & Duties of Prime Minister

Article 77 : Conduct of Business of GoI
  • 77(1): All executive actions of Government of India are taken in the name of the President.
  • 77(2): Orders and instruments made in President's name authenticated under rules.
  • 77(3): President makes rules for convenient transaction of business (*Allocation of Business Rules*).
Article 78 : Duties of the Prime Minister
  • 78(a): Communicate to the President all decisions of the Council of Ministers.
  • 78(b): Furnish information relating to administration and legislation as President calls for.
  • 78(c): Submit for consideration of Council any matter decided by a Minister but not considered by the Council.

💬 8. Powers and Functions of the PM & Eminent Quotes

Core Powers and Functions of the PM
  • 1. Recommends appointments and dismissal of Ministers to the President.
  • 2. Allocates and reshuffles portfolios among Ministers.
  • 3. Presides over meetings of the Cabinet/Council of Ministers.
  • 4. Advises President to summon, prorogue or dissolve the Lok Sabha.
  • 5. Principal spokesperson of the Union Government in Parliament.
  • 6. Resignation of PM brings about the collapse of the entire Ministry.
Scholar Quotes on the Post of PM
Dr. B. R. Ambedkar:
"Collective responsibility can be achieved only through the instrumentality of the Prime Minister."
Pt. Jawaharlal Nehru:
Described PM as "The Linchpin of Government" (सरकार की धुरी).
Lord Morley:
"Primus inter pares" (First among equals).
