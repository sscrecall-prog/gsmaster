Important Historical Acts (1773 to 1947)

📜 1. Historical Background: Company Rule (1773–1858) vs Crown Rule (1858–1947)

British came to India in 1600 as traders in the form of East India Company (EIC) under a charter granted by Queen Elizabeth I. After the Battle of Buxar (1764), EIC obtained Diwani Rights (revenue & civil justice over Bengal, Bihar & Orissa in 1765).

1. The Company Rule (1773–1858)
  • • Regulating Act of 1773 (Centralisation begins)
  • • Amending Act of 1781 (Act of Settlement)
  • • Pitt's India Act of 1784 (Double Government)
  • • Charter Act of 1793
  • • Charter Act of 1813 (End of trade monopoly except Tea/China)
  • • Charter Act of 1833 (GGI Lord Bentinck & Centralisation climax)
  • • Charter Act of 1853 (Open competition in Civil Services)
2. The Crown Rule (1858–1947)
  • • Government of India Act, 1858 (Queen's Proclamation / Viceroy)
  • • Indian Councils Act, 1861 (Decentralisation & Portfolio)
  • • Indian Councils Act, 1909 (Morley-Minto / Separate Electorates)
  • • Government of India Act, 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford / Dyarchy)
  • • Government of India Act, 1935 (Provincial Autonomy & Federal Court)
  • • Indian Independence Act, 1947 (Partition & Sovereign Dominions)

🏛️ 2. Regulating Act 1773, Amending Act 1781 & Pitt's India Act 1784

Regulating Act of 1773
  • 1st step by British Govt to regulate and control EIC affairs.
  • Governor of Bengal designated as Governor-General of Bengal (GGB).
  • Created Executive Council of 4 members to assist GGB.
  • 1st Governor-General of Bengal: Warren Hastings.
  • Bombay and Madras Governors made subordinate to GGB.
  • Established Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774): 1 Chief Justice (Sir Elijah Impey) + 3 Judges.
  • Prohibited private trade & bribes for Company servants.
  • Court of Directors (COD) to report revenue & civil affairs.
Pitt's India Act of 1784 (Double Govt)
  • Named after British PM William Pitt the Younger.
  • System of Double Government (द्वैध शासन):
    • Court of Directors (COD): Commercial affairs.
    • Board of Control (BOC): Civil, military & revenue affairs.
  • Known as "Half Loaf System" (mediated between Crown and EIC).
  • For the 1st time, EIC territories were termed "British Possessions in India".
  • Amending Act 1781 (Act of Settlement): Exempted GGB and council from SC jurisdiction for official acts.

🚢 3. Charter Acts of 1793 & 1813 (Tea Trade & Christian Missionaries)

Charter Act of 1793
  • Extended company's trade monopoly in India for 20 more years.
  • Commander-in-Chief not to be member of council unless appointed.
  • Salaries of Board of Control staff to be paid out of Indian Revenues (Home Charges).
Charter Act of 1813 (Key Exam Highlights)
  • Abolished trade monopoly of EIC in India (Trade opened to all British merchants).
  • ⭐ Monopoly Retained ONLY in: Tea Trade & Trade with China.
  • Allowed Christian Missionaries to come to India to promote English & Christianity.
  • Allotted ₹1 Lakh per year for promotion of Indian Education.
  • Authorized local governments to levy taxes.

⚖️ 4. Charter Acts of 1833 (Saint Helena Act) & 1853 (Open Competition)

Charter Act of 1833 (Saint Helena Act)
  • Final step towards Centralisation in British India.
  • Governor-General of Bengal converted to Governor-General of India (GGI).
  • 1st Governor-General of India: Lord William Bentinck.
  • Deprived Bombay & Madras of their legislative powers.
  • Ended commercial activities of EIC (Purely administrative body; tea/China monopoly ended).
  • Added a Law Member to Council (Lord Macaulay).
  • 1st Law Commission (1834) under Macaulay ➔ Led to IPC (1860).
Charter Act of 1853 (Mini-Parliament)
  • Separated Legislative and Executive functions of GGI Council for the 1st time.
  • Created 6-member Indian (Central) Legislative Council.
  • Introduced Local Representation (4 members from Madras, Bombay, Bengal, Agra).
  • Introduced Open Competitive Examination for Civil Services (ICS) (Macaulay Committee, 1854).
  • ⭐ 1st Indian to qualify Civil Services: Satyendranath Tagore (1863).

👑 5. GoI Act 1858 (Crown Rule) & Indian Councils Act 1861

Government of India Act, 1858 (Queen's Proclamation)
  • Enacted after 1857 Revolt (*"Act for the Good Government of India"*).
  • Abolished East India Company; transferred power directly to British Crown.
  • Designation changed: GGI ➔ Viceroy of India.
  • 1st Viceroy of India (and last GGI): Lord Canning.
  • Abolished Double Government (Abolished BOC & COD).
  • Created Secretary of State for India (SOS) with a 15-member Council.
  • Peel Commission (1858): Reorganized Indian army post-revolt.
Indian Councils Act, 1861 (Decentralisation)
  • Associated Indians with law-making: In 1862, Lord Canning nominated 3 Indians to Legislative Council:
    1. Raja of Benaras
    2. Maharaja of Patiala
    3. Sir Dinkar Rao
  • Initiated Decentralisation by restoring legislative powers to Bombay & Madras.
  • Statutory recognition to Portfolio System (Lord Canning, 1859).
  • Empowered Viceroy to issue Ordinances (valid for 6 months).

🗳️ 6. Indian Councils Act, 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms)

Named after Lord Morley (Secretary of State) & Lord Minto (Viceroy):
  • 1. Central Legislative Council size increased from 16 to 60 members.
  • 2. Members allowed to ask supplementary questions and move resolutions on budget.
  • 3. 1st Indian in Viceroy's Executive Council: Satyendra Prasad Sinha (S.P. Sinha) (Appointed as Law Member).
  • 4. ⭐ Introduced Separate Electorate for Muslims (Communal representation).
  • 5. Lord Minto became known as the "Father of Communal Electorate".

⚡ 7. Government of India Act, 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms)

Dyarchy in Provinces (द्वैध शासन)

Provincial subjects divided into 2 categories:

  • • Transferred Subjects (हस्तांतरित): Administered by Governor with responsible ministers (*Health, Education, Local Self-Gov, Agriculture*).
  • • Reserved Subjects (आरक्षित): Administered by Governor & Executive Council without responsibility (*Police, Justice, Prison, Revenue, Finance*).
Bicameralism & Direct Elections
  • • Introduced Bicameralism at Centre (Council of State & Legislative Assembly) and Direct Elections for the 1st time.
  • • 3 of 6 members of Viceroy's Council to be Indian.
  • • Created High Commissioner for India in London.
  • • Central PSC set up in 1926 (Lee Commission 1924).
  • • Separated Provincial Budget from Central Budget.
  • • Extended separate electorates to Sikhs, Christians, Anglo-Indians & Europeans.

🏛️ 8. Government of India Act, 1935 (Blueprint of Constitution)

Enforced on 1st April 1937. Contained 321 Sections and 10 Schedules. Served as the major structural blueprint for the 1950 Indian Constitution.

1. Provincial Autonomy & Centre Dyarchy
  • • Abolished Dyarchy in Provinces; introduced Provincial Autonomy.
  • • Provided for Dyarchy at Centre (Never came into operation).
  • • Provided for All India Federation (Never formed).
  • • Bicameralism in 6 of 11 Provinces (Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, UP).
2. 3 Lists & Institutions Created
  • • 3 Lists: Federal (59), Provincial (54), Concurrent (36). Residuary powers given to Viceroy.
  • • Established Federal Court in 1937 (CJ: Sir Maurice Gwyer).
  • • Established Reserve Bank of India (RBI - 1935).
  • • Created FPSC, Provincial PSC & Joint PSC.
  • • Separated Burma (Myanmar) from India.
  • • Abolished Council of India (1858).

🇮🇳 9. Indian Independence Act, 1947 & Mountbatten Plan

Clement Attlee Declaration (20 Feb 1947) & Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947):
  • 1. Ended British rule; declared India Independent & Sovereign from 15th August 1947.
  • 2. Partition of India into two independent Dominions: India and Pakistan.
  • 3. Abolished office of Viceroy; provided for Governor-General for each dominion.
  • 4. Abolished Secretary of State for India.
  • 5. Lapsed British Paramountcy over Princely States (free to join India, Pakistan or remain independent).
  • 6. Constituent Assemblies empowered to frame constitutions and repeal British acts.
  • 7. Dropped title of *"Emperor of India"* from British Crown.
