भाग-3 : मूल अधिकार (अनुच्छेद 12 से 35)
🏛️ Magna Carta of India ⚖️ 6 Fundamental Rights 📜 5 Types of Constitutional Writs ⚡ Landmark Supreme Court Precedents

📜 1. PART III: Fundamental Rights [Article 12-35]

Definition of Fundamental Rights:

Fundamental rights are a set of legally enforceable rights essential for the all-round development of individuals.

मूल अधिकार ऐसे अधिकारों का समूह हैं जो व्यक्ति के भौतिक, बौद्धिक, नैतिक और आध्यात्मिक विकास के लिए अपरिहार्य हैं तथा न्यायपालिका द्वारा कानूनी रूप से प्रवर्तनीय हैं।
Salient Features of Fundamental Rights
  • They are not absolute (subject to reasonable restrictions). ये असीमित/निरपेक्ष नहीं हैं (इन पर उचित सीमाएं/प्रतिबंध लागू हैं)।
  • They are not 'Sacrosanct' or Permanent (Parliament can curtail them by constitutional amendment). ये स्थायी या अनुल्लंघनीय नहीं हैं (संसद इनमें संशोधन कर सकती है)।
  • They can be curtailed during emergency (but Article 20 and 21 can never be suspended). आपातकाल में इन्हें निलंबित किया जा सकता है (परंतु अनुच्छेद 20 और 21 कभी निलंबित नहीं होते)।
  • They are justiciable in nature (enforceable by courts). ये न्यायोचित/वादयोग्य हैं (उल्लंघन पर सीधे न्यायालय जा सकते हैं)।
Basic Core Pointers
  • Borrowed from USA Constitution (Bill of Rights). अमेरिका के संविधान (बिल ऑफ राइट्स) से प्रेरित।
  • Contained in Part III : Articles 12–35. संविधान के भाग III (अनुच्छेद 12 से 35) में निहित।
  • Termed as "Conscience of the Constitution". संविधान की 'अंतरात्मा' के रूप में प्रसिद्ध।
  • Seek to implement Political Democracy. राजनीतिक लोकतंत्र के आदर्श की स्थापना करते हैं।
  • Referred to as the "Magna Carta of India". इन्हें भारत का 'मैग्नाकार्टा' (अधिकार पत्र) कहा जाता है।
📌 44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978 (Right to Property):

Originally there were 7 Fundamental Rights. Currently there are 6 Fundamental Rights. Right to Property was removed from Part III in 1978 by the 44th Amendment. It is now a Legal/Constitutional Right under Article 300-A in Part XII (Finance, Property, Contracts and Suits).

मूल संविधान में 7 मूल अधिकार थे। 44वें संविधान संशोधन 1978 द्वारा 'संपत्ति के अधिकार' (अनुच्छेद 31 व 19(1)(f)) को मूल अधिकारों से हटाकर भाग XII के अनुच्छेद 300-A के तहत केवल एक विधिक/कानूनी अधिकार बना दिया गया।

Six Fundamental Rights in the Indian Constitution (वर्तमान में 6 मूल अधिकार):

1. Right to Equality
Articles 14 – 18
समता / समानता का अधिकार
2. Right to Freedom
Articles 19 – 22
स्वतंत्रता का अधिकार
3. Right against Exploitation
Articles 23 – 24
शोषण के विरुद्ध अधिकार
4. Freedom of Religion
Articles 25 – 28
धर्म की स्वतंत्रता का अधिकार
5. Cultural & Educational
Articles 29 – 30
संस्कृति एवं शिक्षा संबंधी अधिकार
6. Constitutional Remedies
Article 32
संवैधानिक उपचारों का अधिकार

🏛️ 2. Articles 12 & 13 (Definition of State & Judicial Review)

Article 12 : Definition of State (राज्य की परिभाषा)

Defines the term "State" for Part III, which includes:

  • 1. Government & Parliament of India (Union Executive & Legislature)
  • 2. Government & Legislature of each of the States
  • 3. All local authorities (Municipalities, Panchayats, District Boards)
  • 4. Other statutory/non-statutory authorities (e.g. PSUs, LIC, ONGC, SAIL, etc.)
मूल अधिकार राज्य के विरुद्ध प्राप्त हैं; अतः केंद्र/राज्य सरकारें, संसद, विधानमंडल, स्थानीय निकाय तथा एलआईसी, ओएनजीसी जैसे सार्वजनिक उपक्रम 'राज्य' के दायरे में आते हैं।
Article 13 : Laws Inconsistent with Fundamental Rights

Expressly provides for the doctrine of Judicial Review (न्यायिक समीक्षा):

  • Any pre-constitutional or post-constitutional law that violates Fundamental Rights shall be declared null and void by the judiciary.
  • "Law" includes: Ordinances, orders, bye-laws, rules, regulations, notifications, customs having force of law.
  • Article 13 states it does not apply to amendments under Article 368 (subject to Kesavananda Bharati basic structure doctrine).

⚖️ 3. Right to Equality (Articles 14 to 18)

Article 14 : Equality before Law & Equal Protection of Laws

The State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India.

Equality Before Law (विधि के समक्ष समता):
  • Negative in nature (British Concept).
  • No one is above the law; no special privileges.
  • Rule of Law (विधि का शासन): Propounded by British Jurist A.V. Dicey.
Equal Protection of Law (विधियों का समान संरक्षण):
  • Positive in nature (American Concept).
  • Equal treatment under equal circumstances (like should be treated alike).
Exceptions to Article 14: President & Governors (Art 361 immunity from criminal court proceedings), MPs/MLAs (Arts 105 & 194 freedom of speech in House), Foreign diplomats/ambassadors and UNO.

Article 15 : Prohibition of Discrimination (5 Grounds)

No discrimination on grounds ONLY of Religion, Race, Caste, Sex, or Place of Birth.

15 (1): State shall not discriminate against any citizen on only 5 grounds.
15 (2): Equal access to shops, restaurants, hotels, wells, tanks, bathing ghats, public roads.
15 (3): Special provisions for women and children (e.g. reserved metro seats, free education).
15 (4): Special provisions for advancement of SC, ST and SEBC (OBCs) (Added by 1st CA 1951).

⚖️ Champakam Dorairajan Case (1951)

  • Champakam Dorairajan challenged Madras Communal Government Order for college admissions as violation of Article 14 & 15(1).
  • Supreme Court Verdict (1951): Struck down the reservation order. Held that Fundamental Rights prevail over DPSPs.
  • Result: Led to the 1st Constitutional Amendment Act 1951, which added Article 15(4) enabling reservations for backward classes.

Article 16 : Equality of Opportunity in Public Employment

• 16 (1): Equality of opportunity in public appointments.

• 16 (2): No discrimination on 7 grounds: Religion, Race, Caste, Sex, Descent, Place of Birth, Residence.

• 16 (3): Parliament can prescribe residential requirement for certain posts.

• 16 (4): State can make reservations for backward classes not adequately represented.

Commissions & Landmark Case on Article 16(4):
  • Kalelkar Commission (1953): 1st Backward Class Commission (Kaka Kalelkar).
  • Mandal Commission (1979): 2nd Backward Class Commission (B.P. Mandal) ➔ Recommended 27% reservation for OBCs (implemented in 1990 by PM V.P. Singh).
  • Indra Sawhney Case (1992) (Mandal Case): Upheld 27% OBC quota; ruled that total reservation must not exceed 50% cap; introduced "Creamy Layer" exclusion.
  • Public Interest Litigation (PIL): Concept borrowed from USA.
Article 17 : Abolition of Untouchability

Untouchability is abolished and its practice in any form is forbidden. Note: The term 'Untouchability' is nowhere defined in the Constitution.

Article 18 : Abolition of Titles

No titles other than military & academic distinctions (Bharat Ratna/Padma awards are not titles). Abolished colonial titles like Maharaja, Rai Bahadur.

🕊️ 4. Right to Freedom (Articles 19 to 22)

Article 19 : Protection of 6 Fundamental Freedoms

(1)(a) Speech & Expression (वाक् व अभिव्यक्ति) (1)(b) Peaceful Assembly without arms (शांतिपूर्ण सम्मेलन) (1)(c) Associations & Co-operatives (संघ व समितियां) (1)(d) Free Movement (अबाध संचरण) (1)(e) Reside & Settle (निवास) (1)(g) Trade / Profession (व्यापार व आजीविका)

💡 Trick: `S A A M R O` (Speech, Assembly, Association, Movement, Residence, Occupation).
• 97th CA (2011): Added Right to form Cooperative Societies under Art 19(1)(c).
• 44th CA (1978): Right to Property (19(1)(f)) was omitted.

Implicit Rights & Precedents under Article 19(1)(a):

• Right to Internet: Anuradha Bhasin v. UOI (2020)

• Right to Information: Raj Narain v. State of UP (1975)

• Freedom of Press: Romesh Thappar v. Madras (1950)

• Sec 66A IT Act Struck Down: Shreya Singhal v. UOI (2015)

Article 20 : Protection in Respect of Conviction for Offences

(1) No Ex-Post-Facto Law: Cannot be punished under retroactive criminal laws.
(2) No Double Jeopardy: Cannot be prosecuted and punished twice for the same offence.
(3) No Self-Incrimination: Cannot be compelled to be a witness against oneself (blood/thumb allowed).
Article 21(A) : Right to Education (RTE)

Added by 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002.
Guarantees free and compulsory education for children aged 6 to 14 years as a Fundamental Right.

Article 22 : Protection against Arrest & Detention

• Punitive: Informed of grounds, consult lawyer, produced before magistrate in 24 hours.
• Preventive Detention: Max 3 months (extension only via HC Advisory Board).

⚡ 5. Article 21 & Landmark Precedents (Right to Life & Personal Liberty)

Article 21 : Protection of Life and Personal Liberty

"No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law."
• "Procedure established by law" borrowed from Japan.
• Articles 20 and 21 can NEVER be suspended during emergency.

Landmark Cases & Evolution of Article 21:

A.K. Gopalan Case (1950)

Narrow interpretation of Art 21. Ruled that law need only follow procedure, not 'Due Process'.

Maneka Gandhi Case (1978)

Overturned Gopalan; introduced American "Due Process of Law" (law must be just, fair and reasonable); Right to Travel Abroad.

K.S. Puttaswamy Case (2017)

9-judge bench unanimously affirmed Right to Privacy as an intrinsic fundamental right under Article 21.

Olga Tellis Case (1985)

Right to Livelihood is an essential part of the Right to Life.

Hussainara Khatoon Case (1979)

Right to Speedy Trial and Free Legal Aid for undertrials.

Bachan Singh (1980) & Aruna Shanbaug (2011)

Death penalty in "Rarest of Rare Cases"; Legalized Passive Euthanasia under strict medical guidelines.

🛑 6. Rights against Exploitation, Religion & Minorities (Arts 23–30)

Right against Exploitation (Arts 23–24)
  • Article 23: Prohibition of Human Trafficking and Begar (Forced Labour). (Exception: compulsory state service for public purpose).
  • Article 24: Prohibition of child labour below the age of 14 years in hazardous places (factories, mines).
Freedom of Religion (Arts 25–28)
  • Article 25: Freedom of conscience, practice, propagation (Sikh Kirpans allowed; forcible conversions prohibited).
  • Article 26: Freedom to manage religious affairs & own property.
  • Article 27: No taxes for promoting any particular religion.
  • Article 28: Religious instructions prohibited in state-funded schools.
Cultural & Educational Rights of Minorities (Arts 29–30)
Article 29: Any section of citizens has right to conserve distinct language, script, or culture (Applies to all citizens, not just minorities).
Article 30: Religious and linguistic minorities have the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice. (Term 'Minority' is not defined in Constitution).

📜 7. Article 32 & 5 Types of Constitutional Writs

"Heart and Soul of the Constitution" — Dr. B. R. Ambedkar

Article 32 provides the Right to Constitutional Remedies. Without Article 32, other Fundamental Rights would be meaningless. The Supreme Court can issue 5 types of Writs for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

1. Habeas Corpus

Meaning: "To have the body of"
Protects against unlawful detention. Issued against public officials as well as private individuals (ADM Jabalpur case).

2. Mandamus

Meaning: "We command"
Orders a public official to perform statutory duty. Cannot be issued against President, Governors, or private individuals.

3. Prohibition

Meaning: "To forbid"
Preventive in nature. Issued by higher court to stop lower court from exceeding jurisdiction.

4. Certiorari

Meaning: "To be certified"
Curative & preventive. Quashes order of lower court/tribunal delivered without jurisdiction.

5. Quo-Warranto

Meaning: "By what authority or warrant"
Inquires into legality of claim of a person to a public office to prevent illegal usurpation of public office.

Writ Jurisdiction: Supreme Court (Art 32) vs High Court (Art 226)

Feature Supreme Court (Article 32) High Court (Article 226)
Nature of Remedy Article 32 is itself a Fundamental Right (SC cannot refuse to issue writs). Article 226 is a discretionary remedy (HC can refuse to issue writs).
Scope / Jurisdiction Only for enforcement of Fundamental Rights (Narrower scope). For Fundamental Rights + Ordinary Legal Rights (Wider jurisdiction).
Territorial Reach Throughout the territory of India. Throughout its State territory or where cause of action arises.

🛡️ 8. Articles 33–35 & Rights Available Only to Indian Citizens

Article 33

Parliament can restrict Fundamental Rights of Armed Forces, Police & Intelligence agencies (50th CA 1984).

Article 34

Restrictions on Fundamental Rights while Martial Law is in force (Borrowed from UK).

Article 35

Vests exclusive power in Parliament to make laws to enforce specified fundamental rights.

🇮🇳 Fundamental Rights Available ONLY to Indian Citizens (Not to Foreigners):
Article 15 Article 16 Article 19 Article 29 Article 30

(Articles 14, 20, 21, 21A, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28 are available to both citizens and foreigners, except enemy aliens).
