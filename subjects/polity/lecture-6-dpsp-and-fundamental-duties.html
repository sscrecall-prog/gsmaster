📜 1. Salient Features of DPSP & Scholar Comments

Salient Features of DPSP (नीति निदेशक तत्व)
  • DPSP are non-justiciable, means that they are not legally enforceable by courts. DPSP गैर-न्यायोचित (अवादयोग्य) हैं, अर्थात न्यायालय द्वारा इन्हें लागू नहीं कराया जा सकता।
  • The concept is borrowed from the Irish Constitution (Ireland). यह विचार आयरलैंड के संविधान (1937) से लिया गया है।
  • Their objective is to help establish Social and Economic Democracy. इनका मुख्य उद्देश्य देश में सामाजिक एवं आर्थिक लोकतंत्र की स्थापना करना है।
  • DPSPs reflect the vision of a Welfare State as outlined in the Constitution. ये संविधान में उल्लिखित कल्याणकारी राज्य (Welfare State) की परिकल्पना को साकार करते हैं।
  • They serve as a measure to assess laws based on their alignment with constitutional ideals. ये कानूनों की संवैधानिक आदर्शों के साथ अनुकूलता की जांच करने का पैमाना हैं।
  • Novel Features / Resemble the Instrument of Instructions as contained in GoI Act 1935: Dr. B. R. Ambedkar. डॉ. आंबेडकर ने इन्हें संविधान की 'अनोखी विशेषता' तथा भारत शासन अधिनियम 1935 के 'इंस्ट्रूमेंट ऑफ इंस्ट्रक्शंस' के समान बताया।
  • DPSPs have Moral and Political sanctions. DPSP के पीछे नैतिक एवं राजनीतिक जनमत की शक्ति है।

🏛️ 2. Articles 36 to 39 (Definition, Application, Welfare & Key Principles)

Article 36 : Definition of State (राज्य की परिभाषा)
  • Defines the term "State" as it is used in Part III.
  • The meaning of "State" here is the same as in Article 12 under Fundamental Rights (Govt, Parliament, State Govts, Legislatures, Local Authorities, PSUs).
Article 37 : Application of the Principles (तत्वों का लागू होना)
  • They are non-justiciable in nature (न्यायालय द्वारा अप्रवर्तनीय).
  • It is the duty of the State to apply these principles in making laws; these are fundamental in the governance of the country.
Article 38 : State to Secure a Social Order for the Welfare of the People
  • (a) The State must promote people's welfare by securing a social order permeated by Justice — Social, Economic, and Political.
  • (b) The State shall minimize inequalities in income, status, facilities, and opportunities (Added by 44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978).
Article 39 : Certain Principles of Policy to be Followed by the State (6 Directives)

• (a) Adequate means of Livelihood for all citizens (जीविका के पर्याप्त साधन)।

• (b) Fair distribution of material resources for the common good (संसाधनों का लोकहित में वितरण)।

• (c) Avoiding concentration of wealth and production to common detriment (धन का अकेंद्रीकरण)।

• (d) Equal pay for equal work for both men and women (समान कार्य के लिए समान वेतन)।

• (e) Protection of workers' health and children from exploitation (स्वास्थ्य व शक्ति का संरक्षण)।

• (f) Proper development opportunities for children in conditions of freedom & dignity (Added by 42nd Amendment, 1976)।

⚖️ 3. Articles 39A to 49 (Detailed Directive Principles)

Article 39A

Equal Justice and Free Legal Aid to the poor & weaker sections (Added by 42nd CA, 1976 - led to NALSA Act 1987).

Article 40

Organisation of Village Panchayats as units of self-government (Gandhian Principle - led to 73rd Amendment).

Article 41

Right to Work, Education, and Public Assistance in cases of unemployment, old age, sickness and disablement (e.g. MGNREGA).

Article 42

Just and Humane Working Conditions & Maternity Relief (काम की न्यायसंगत दशाएं व प्रसूति सहायता - Maternity Benefit Act).

Article 43

Living Wages and Decent Working Conditions, promotion of cottage industries in rural areas on individual/cooperative basis.

Article 43A

Workers' Participation in Management of Industries (Added by 42nd CA, 1976).

Article 43B

Promotion of Cooperative Societies — voluntary formation, autonomous functioning (Added by 97th CA, 2011).

Article 44

Uniform Civil Code (UCC) — The State shall endeavour to secure for the citizens a uniform civil code throughout the territory of India.

Article 45

Early Childhood Care and Education for children below 6 years (Subject matter changed by 86th CA, 2002 after Art 21A).

Article 46

Promotion of Educational and Economic Interests of SCs, STs, and Weaker Sections, and protection from social injustice.

Article 47

Duty to Raise Nutrition, Living Standards & Public Health; Prohibit consumption of intoxicating drinks and drugs.

Article 48

Organisation of Agriculture and Animal Husbandry on modern scientific lines; Prohibit slaughter of cows, calves, and useful cattle.

Article 48A

Protection of Environment, Forests, and Wildlife (Added by 42nd CA, 1976).

Article 49

Protection of Monuments and Places of National Importance declared by Parliament.

🕊️ 4. Articles 50 & 51 (Separation of Judiciary & International Peace)

Article 50 : Separation of Judiciary from Executive

Separation of the judiciary from the executive in the public services of the State to ensure independence of the judicial system.

राज्य की लोक सेवाओं में न्यायपालिका को कार्यपालिका से पृथक करना।

Article 51 : Promotion of International Peace and Security

Promote international peace & security; maintain just and honourable relations; foster respect for international law; encourage settlement of disputes by arbitration.

अंतर्राष्ट्रीय शांति और सुरक्षा की अभिवृद्धि तथा राष्ट्रों के बीच न्यायसंगत व सम्मानपूर्ण संबंधों को बढ़ावा देना।

🗂️ 5. Three-Fold Classification of DPSPs

⚠️ Note: This classification of DPSPs into Socialist, Gandhian, and Liberal-Intellectual is NOT mentioned in the Constitution (it is an ideological categorization by scholars).
1. Socio-Economic / Socialistic

Aim for social & economic welfare, equality and welfare state.

Articles: 38, 39, 39A, 41, 42, 43, 43A, 47
2. Gandhian Principles

Decentralized governance, cottage industries, self-sufficiency, prohibition.

Articles: 40, 43, 43B, 46, 47, 48
3. Liberal-Intellectual

Individual freedoms, UCC, rational state, environment, international peace.

Articles: 44, 45, 48, 48A, 49, 50, 51

⚡ 6. DPSPs Added Through Constitutional Amendments

42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 (Added 4 Directives)

• Article 39(f): Children's healthy development

• Article 39A: Equal justice & free legal aid to poor

• Article 43A: Participation of workers in management

• Article 48A: Protect environment & safeguard wildlife

44th CA (1978)

Article 38(2): State to minimize inequalities in income, status, facilities.

86th CA (2002)

Article 45: Early childhood care and education for children below 6 years.

97th CA (2011)

Article 43B: Promotion of voluntary formation of Cooperative Societies.

⚖️ 7. Fundamental Rights (FRs) vs DPSPs: Cases & Differences

1951

Champakam Dorairajan

FRs prevail over DPSPs. FRs are amendable.

1967

Golaknath Case

Parliament cannot revoke/amend FRs for DPSPs. Led to 25th CA (Art 31C).

1973

Kesavananda Bharati

Upheld Art 31C (39b/c over 14/19), but struck down clause barring judicial review.

1980

Minerva Mills Case

Constitution is founded on the bedrock of balance between FRs and DPSPs.

Key Differences: Fundamental Rights vs DPSPs

Feature Fundamental Rights (Part III) DPSPs (Part IV)
Nature of Obligation Negative obligations (prohibits state action). Positive obligations (urges state action).
Enforceability Justiciable (enforceable by courts). Non-justiciable (not legally enforceable).
Democracy Goal Establishes Political Democracy. Establishes Social & Economic Democracy.
Sanction Behind Protected by legal sanctions. Backed by moral and political sanctions.
Focus / Scope Oriented towards individual welfare. Focused on societal / community welfare.
Implementation Automatically enforceable; laws violating FRs are void. Require legislative enactment; courts cannot invalidate laws violating DPSP.

🛡️ 8. PART IVA — Fundamental Duties [Article 51A]

Basic Pointers on Fundamental Duties
  • Borrowed from the USSR (Soviet Union).
  • Statutory and non-enforceable / non-justiciable (unless specific statutes enforce them).
  • Not originally part of the Constitution (added later).
  • Applicable ONLY to Indian citizens, not to foreigners.
  • Introduced through the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976.
  • Initially 10 Fundamental Duties were added in 1976.
  • Currently there are 11 Fundamental Duties.
  • The 11th duty [51A(k)] was added by the 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002.
⭐ Sardar Swaran Singh Committee

On the recommendation of the Sardar Swaran Singh Committee (1976), Fundamental Duties were added to the Indian Constitution.

📋 9. The 11 Fundamental Duties [Article 51A(a) to (k)]

(a) Constitution, Flag & Anthem

To abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag and the National Anthem.

(b) Cherish Noble Ideals

To cherish and follow the noble ideals that inspired the national struggle for freedom.

(c) Sovereignty, Unity & Integrity

To uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India.

(d) Defend Country & National Service

To defend the country and render national service when called upon to do so.

(e) Harmony, Brotherhood & Women's Dignity

To promote harmony and common brotherhood; to renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women.

(f) Value & Preserve Rich Heritage

To value and preserve the rich heritage of our composite culture.

(g) Protect Natural Environment

To protect and improve forests, lakes, rivers and wildlife, and to have compassion for living creatures.

(h) Scientific Temper & Humanism

To develop the scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform.

(i) Safeguard Public Property & Abjure Violence

To safeguard public property and to abjure violence.

(j) Strive Towards Excellence

To strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity.

(k) Opportunity for Children's Education (6 to 14 Years)

Duty of a parent or guardian to provide opportunities for education to his child or ward between the age of six and fourteen years. (Added through 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002).

⚖️ 10. Justice Verma Committee (1999) & Enforcing Laws

Justice Verma Committee (1999):

Identified the existence of legal provisions for the implementation of Fundamental Duties and recommended creating awareness and teaching school children about their duties.

Laws Reflecting and Enforcing Fundamental Duties:

UAPA Act (1967): Prevents activities threatening sovereignty & integrity ➔ Duty (c).
Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act (1971): Respect for Flag & Anthem ➔ Duty (a).
Wildlife Protection Act (1972): Protects wild animals and birds ➔ Duty (g).
Representation of the People Act (1951): Regulates democratic electoral process ➔ Duty (a).
Indian Penal Code (IPC / BNS): Enforces public order, bans acts derogatory to women ➔ Duties (e) & (i).
Protection of Civil Rights Act (1955): Eliminates untouchability, promotes equality ➔ Duty (e).
